1 · The idea in 60 seconds

The planner is a servo — Maxwell Maltz's word (Psycho-Cybernetics) for a self-correcting guidance system. You give it a target picture of who you're becoming (four goal areas, rated weekly). You live your day in blocks. It measures the gap between target and behavior every night, then makes small automatic corrections toward the target — like backpropagation, one nudge at a time. Every correction is visible in the Training log. Nothing changes silently.

It also carries the other two books: rotating first-person identity lines all day (Murphy — repeated impression), and two short rituals at the day's open and close, when the mind is most impressionable (Murphy + Dispenza — feel the future as already real).

What makes it more than a planner: two scheduled Claude routines — running on your own subscription, no API keys — close the loop through your real life. At night one measures your day and proposes tomorrow; each morning the other lays that day onto your calendar and phone. You wake up to a day already built around who you're becoming, and you stay in control: it proposes, you approve.

2 · Your daily rhythm — three touches

1Morning (2–3 min). Open the planner → tap Morning prime. Read who you are, mentally rehearse the day going well (the rehearsal is aimed at whatever needs you most), feel it as already true, set today's ONE thing.
2During the day. Live the blocks. Tap the checkbox when one is done — that's the entire interaction. The sticky bar at the top always shows your current identity line.
3Night (2–3 min). Tap Pre-sleep. Review the day as feedback (never a verdict), write one honest line, tap the line that carried you, optionally run the Claude loop, and fall asleep on tonight's affirmation.

The two rituals are worth 10 points each of your daily Alignment score — the system treats them as seriously as the blocks.

3 · Working with blocks

  • Complete: tap the checkbox. Undo by tapping again.
  • Resize: −15 / +15 buttons. Times downstream recompute automatically — there are never gaps.
  • Rename: tap the title, type, press Enter.
  • Type: tap the colored type chip to cycle (Deep Work → Break → Learn → Exercise → Leisure → Wind-down).
  • Goal tag: tap the ◇ chip to tie a block to one of your four goals — this is how the system knows which goal your behavior is serving.
  • Reorder: drag (desktop) or the ↑↓ arrows.
  • Capture: a stray thought mid-block? Park it in the Capture box and stay in the block.

4 · The automatic loop (the cybernetic part)

Two scheduled Claude routines run every day on your subscription and keep the planner in sync with your real life through a small shared cloud store. They sense what you actually did and act in the real world — but they only ever propose changes; you approve them.

🌙Evening — 22:00 IST. Reads your day, scores your Alignment, notices your weak hours, then reads tomorrow's real meetings (Google Calendar) and due tasks (Asana). It writes you a personal coaching note and proposes tomorrow's plan. All of it is waiting in the app when you do your pre-sleep ritual.
☀️Morning — 07:00 IST. Takes the plan you approved (or auto-applies it with a one-tap revert if you didn't get to it), puts every block on your Google Calendar with popup reminders, pre-plants phone pings in your weak hours, and emails you a morning digest. You wake at 07:30 to a day already set up.

You stay in charge. At pre-sleep the app shows a proposal card — Approve, Apply now, or Dismiss. If a morning plan was auto-applied, the card offers a one-tap Revert. The routines never mark blocks done, never touch your XP or streak — those are your real actions. And the whole thing degrades gracefully: no sync key → the planner just works offline as before; a routine can't reach a connector → it still writes your plan and pings.

The sync chip (top-right of the board) tells you the state: ● synced · ● syncing · ● offline. No chip at all = no sync key set (pure local mode).

There's also an async coach card: leave it a message any time (“am I overloading Tuesdays?”) and the next routine answers inside the app. The “Go deeper tonight” button copies today's snapshot and opens claude.ai for a live conversation.

5 · The Claude coaching (the manual bridge)

Even without the routines, you can coach any night by hand with your Claude.ai subscription — no API keys. You are the bridge:

1In the Pre-sleep flow, tap Copy Today card. It packages your day — blocks done/missed, alignment, goal ratings, your reflection — with coaching instructions.
2Open your Claude app (phone or desktop), paste, and talk the day through like a coaching session. Claude knows to treat misses as feedback, help you feel the goal as real, and end on a pre-sleep impression.
3When you're done, ask for tomorrow's plan. Claude replies ending in a small ```planner code block.
4Copy Claude's whole reply, paste it into the box in the Pre-sleep flow, tap Apply. Tomorrow's blocks, focus, emphasis, and a fresh affirmation are applied instantly.

What Claude's block can change: tomorrow's blocks, the one focus, which goal the reminders lean toward (for one day), and a new affirmation line. Anything malformed is ignored — a bad paste can never corrupt your plan. And the planner adapts on its own every night regardless; the Claude loop is the richer layer, never a dependency.

6 · Phone reminders (two free ways, no Google Cloud)

The planner can't ring your phone when it's closed — so it uses two free channels that can:

Antfy pushes (best). Install the free ntfy app, pick a secret topic word, and put it in Settings. The morning routine pushes real notifications to that topic — including extra pings pre-planted in your weak hours. Tap Test in Settings to confirm it reaches your phone.
BCalendar file (zero setup). Settings → Calendar reminders (.ics) downloads becoming-reminders.ics. Import it once at calendar.google.com → ⚙ Settings → Import & export (Apple Calendar / Outlook: just open the file). Each identity line becomes a recurring daily notification whose title is the line.

Once the full loop is set up, the morning routine also puts every block on your Google Calendar as a timed event with a popup reminder — via your claude.ai Google Calendar connector, so there's no Google Cloud project or OAuth client to create. The .ics file stays as the always-works fallback.

7 · One-time setup (to switch the loop on)

The planner works fully offline out of the box. These steps light up the automatic loop — do them once. Everything here is free; no Anthropic API key, no Google Cloud, no paid service.

1Cloud store. Vercel dashboard → Marketplace → Upstash Redis (free, no card) → connect to the project. It auto-adds two env vars. Then add one more, PLANNER_SYNC_KEY, set to a long random string you invent → Redeploy.
2Link your devices. Open the planner → ⚙ Settings → paste that same key in Sync key. Do this on every device (phone, laptop). The sync chip turns green.
3Phone pings. Install the ntfy app → subscribe to a secret topic → put the topic in Settings → tap Test. Turn on “pre-plant reminders in my weak hours” if you want them.
4Connectors. In your Claude app → Settings → Connectors → make sure Google Calendar, Gmail, and Asana are connected. (Section 8 below has a ready-made prompt to test them.)
5Distraction sensor (optional). iPhone Shortcuts → Automation → “When Instagram opened” → Get Contents of URL → POST https://bedifutureworks.com/api/planner/event?key=YOUR_KEY, body {"type":"distraction","app":"instagram"} → Run Immediately. Repeat per app. Now the loop learns your weak hours from real life.
6The routines. They're scheduled to run 07:00 and 22:00 IST. They read PLANNER_SYNC_KEY from their environment — make sure it's available to them, and they'll do the rest.

8 · How it learns you (the servo, in plain words)

Every one of these lands in the Training log on the board, with its reason. If the system did it, you can read why.

9 · Streak, shield, XP — evidence, never judgment

  • A clean day = every block done. Clean days build your streak and your lifetime count.
  • One grace shield absorbs a missed day — the streak holds. It refills after 7 clean days.
  • A reset touches only the streak number. Level, XP, and lifetime clean days are permanent.
  • A day you never opened the planner counts as a miss — honestly — and the system moves on. Feedback, not verdict.

10 · The redirect (SOS)

When the pull toward the screen shows up, tap “I feel the pull.” Three steps, one at a time: physical reset → reach a real person → become the man (your identity line + a pleasure-vs-joy reframe). Using the tool earns XP — reaching for it is the win — and the next morning's rehearsal automatically aims at that exact moment.

11 · Your data & backups

  • Without a sync key: everything lives in your browser on that device — no account, no server, no tracking — and each device has its own copy.
  • With a sync key: your state also mirrors to your private cloud store, so phone, laptop, and the routines all see the same plan live. The key itself is stored only on each device and is never included in a backup export.
  • Settings → Export backup downloads a JSON file. Import loads it anywhere — another browser, another device, or the offline copy (becoming-planner.html, a single file that runs the whole planner without internet and speaks the same backup format).
  • Export before clearing browser data. The cloud store is the live mirror, not a long-term archive — keep a JSON backup too.

12 · FAQ

I missed a whole day. Did I break it?

No. The shield absorbs it if you have one; otherwise only the streak number resets. Your level, XP, lifetime days, and everything it has learned about you are untouched.

Why don't I see the same data on my phone and laptop?

Set the same sync key in Settings on both (section 7). Once they share a key, every device — and the nightly routines — read and write the same live plan. Without a key, storage stays per-device; move data with Export → Import instead.

Can Claude write into the planner directly?

No — and that's deliberate. You're on a Claude subscription (no API keys), so the copy-paste is the write step. It also means you consciously read every change before applying it.

The paste didn't apply.

Paste Claude's entire reply — the planner finds the last ```planner block on its own. If Claude didn't produce one, ask: "give me tomorrow's plan in the planner format from my card."

Do the reminders work when the planner is closed?

Yes. Three of the four channels fire with the planner shut: ntfy phone pushes (sent by the morning routine), Google Calendar popups (the routine writes timed events with reminders), and the .ics calendar you imported once. Only the in-app rotation and optional browser notifications need the planner open.

What if a nudge is wrong — it trimmed a block I care about?

Just resize the block back with +15. Your manual choice becomes the new data, and the cooldown stops the system from fighting you. You always outrank the servo.
